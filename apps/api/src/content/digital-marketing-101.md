# The marketing funnel

A marketing funnel describes the journey from "never heard of you" to "paying customer." The classic stages: **awareness** (someone sees your brand), **consideration** (they evaluate you against alternatives), **conversion** (they buy), **retention** (they buy again), **advocacy** (they tell friends). Each stage needs different content and different metrics — awareness optimizes for impressions and reach, conversion for sign-ups and revenue per visitor.

Funnels leak at every stage. If 10,000 people see your ad, 500 click, 50 sign up, and 5 pay, your conversion rates are 5% (click), 10% (signup), 10% (paid). Fixing the leakiest stage gives you the biggest wins. Many founders obsess over driving more traffic when their real problem is a 2% sign-up rate — solving that doubles revenue without spending a rupee more on ads.

# SEO basics

Search Engine Optimization is making your content rank for the queries your customers type into Google. Three pillars: **technical** (page loads fast, mobile-friendly, no broken links, valid HTML), **on-page** (the right keywords in the title, headings, and body without stuffing), and **off-page** (other reputable sites link to you).

Keyword research starts with thinking like your customer. If you sell handmade leather wallets, your prospects don't search "leather wallets" — they search "best leather wallets under 2000," "compact wallet for jeans," or "rfid blocking wallet India." These long-tail queries have lower volume but much higher intent. Tools like Google Keyword Planner, Ahrefs, or even Google's "People Also Ask" panel surface them for free or cheap.

SEO is a 6–12 month game. New domains take time to earn trust. Don't expect results in weeks; expect them in quarters, and only if you publish genuinely useful content consistently.

# Paid ads: Google, Meta, basics

Paid ads buy attention. Two dominant platforms in India: **Google Ads** (capture demand — people searching for what you sell) and **Meta Ads** (create demand — interrupt people on Facebook/Instagram who didn't know they wanted it). They serve different jobs and you usually need both.

The metric chain that matters: CPM (cost per 1000 impressions) → CTR (click rate) → CPC (cost per click) → conversion rate → CAC (customer acquisition cost) → LTV (lifetime value). The business works when LTV is at least 3× CAC. Below 2× and you're losing money on growth.

Start small. Run ₹5,000 across 3–5 ad variations for a week, kill the bottom 60%, and double down on what worked. Most founders waste money by setting wide targeting, running one ad, and refusing to read the data. Ads are a learning machine — feed them attention, read what they tell you, iterate.

# Email and lifecycle marketing

Email is unsexy and outperforms almost everything per rupee spent. You own the list — algorithm changes can't take it away. Three campaign types do most of the work: **welcome sequences** (5–7 emails over 2 weeks teaching new sign-ups how to get value), **abandoned cart** (someone added to cart and left — recover with a reminder + maybe a discount on day 3), **win-back** (lapsed customers — "we miss you" with a personalised offer).

Open rates are increasingly meaningless thanks to Apple's privacy changes auto-loading images. Watch click rates and conversions. Subject lines under 50 characters, personalisation beyond just first name (last purchase, last viewed product), and one clear call to action per email — three competing CTAs is the same as zero.

# Analytics and attribution

If you can't measure it, you can't improve it. The minimum stack: Google Analytics 4 (or a lighter alternative like Plausible) for site behaviour, Meta Pixel and Google Tag for ad attribution, and a CRM (HubSpot free, or just a spreadsheet) for lead tracking.

Attribution is the question "which channel deserves credit for this sale?" The honest answer is "all of them, in some proportion." A customer saw your Instagram ad in January, your tweet in February, googled you in March, clicked the ad, bookmarked the site, came back direct in April, and bought. Different attribution models (first touch, last touch, linear, time decay) give different answers — none is "right." Pick one, stick with it for at least 6 months so trends are comparable, and don't kill a channel based on one month of data.
